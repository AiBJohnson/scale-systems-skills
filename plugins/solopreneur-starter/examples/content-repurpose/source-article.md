# The Friday admin hour

Harbor Ledger is a fictional one-person design studio. Its owner used to handle small administrative tasks whenever they appeared. An unpaid invoice interrupted a design session. A calendar question interrupted lunch. A subscription reminder became another browser tab left open for days.

The studio is testing a simpler routine: one protected review every Friday afternoon. The review is limited to 45 minutes and uses three lists:

1. invoices that need attention;
2. commitments due during the next working week;
3. recurring charges that need a keep-or-cancel decision.

The point is not to automate every decision. The point is to put routine review in one place while keeping money, customer promises and cancellations under human control.

After six test weeks, the owner wrote that Monday felt less surprising. That is a personal observation from this fictional scenario, not measured evidence of saved time or increased revenue. The studio has not compared the routine with another method.

A useful Friday review ends with a small record: what was checked, what still needs judgement and what the owner deliberately chose not to change. If an invoice amount, deadline or subscription term is missing, the record names the gap instead of guessing.

The routine is intentionally modest. It does not promise an empty inbox, automatic bookkeeping or a business that runs without its owner. It creates one repeatable place to notice what requires attention.
