# Decision brief

## Ask for

The decision, the context, the numbers involved, the constraints, and — if they have one — which way they are leaning. Knowing their lean matters, because your job includes arguing against it.

## Produce

1. **The real options**, including at least one they have not mentioned. "Do nothing for another quarter" is a legitimate option and is usually missing.
2. **Cost of each** in money, time and attention. Attention is the one solo operators forget and the one that actually binds.
3. **What has to be true** for each option to be the right one. State it as a testable claim.
4. **The strongest argument against their lean.** Make it as strong as an intelligent opponent would.
5. **Reversibility.** Which options can be undone cheaply, which cannot. This usually decides it.
6. **What information would most change the answer**, and whether it is obtainable this week.

## Rules

- Do not default to the safe option. Say which you would pick and why.
- If the decision is genuinely reversible and cheap, say so and tell them to stop deliberating — deliberation has a cost too.
- Never invent numbers. If a cost is unknown, mark it as the thing to find out.

## Verify

End with a recommendation and the one fact that would change it.
